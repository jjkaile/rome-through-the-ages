The Big Statue

Daniel Chapter 2

Head of Gold

"Thou art this head of gold" — Daniel 2:38

Babylon. 605–539 BC. About 66 years at the top.

Nebuchadnezzar heard Daniel's interpretation... and then apparently missed the point entirely. He built a statue of his own — 90 feet tall, pure gold — and commanded everyone to bow down and worship it.

Gold statue in the dream: you are great, O king.
Gold statue he built: I am great, O everyone.

God humbled him. Drove him mad. He lived like an animal for seven years, eating grass, nails growing like claws — until he finally looked up and acknowledged that heaven rules. (Daniel 4)

He recovered. But his grandson Belshazzar didn't learn the lesson.

Belshazzar threw a party. Drank wine from the sacred temple vessels his grandfather had looted from Jerusalem. That night, a hand appeared on the wall:

That very night, Belshazzar was killed. Babylon fell to the Medes and Persians.

66 years. Head of gold. Gone.

Chest & Arms of Silver

"After you shall arise another kingdom inferior to yours" — Daniel 2:39

Medo-Persia. 539–331 BC. Two hundred years.

Two arms, two shoulders — the Medes and the Persians. Two peoples whose power was united under one man: Cyrus the Great.

Cyrus didn't just conquer Babylon — he walked in almost unopposed. Belshazzar was partying. The Persians diverted the Euphrates River and marched under the city walls. That same night. The night of the writing on the wall.

"Your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians." — Daniel 5:28

Cyrus was different from most conquerors. He freed the Jewish exiles. Let them go home. Let them rebuild the temple. Isaiah had called him by name 150 years before he was born. (Isaiah 45:1)

His tomb still stands today at Pasargadae, Iran. The inscription reads:

"O man, whoever you are, from wherever you come —
for I know you shall come —
I am Cyrus, who gave the Persians their empire.
Do not grudge me this patch of earth
that covers my body."

This patch of earth. That's all that's left.

But Daniel said "inferior." Silver, not gold. Bigger territory, longer reign — and still inferior. The metals go down in value even as the empires go up in power. Something to sit with.

In the end, the last Persian king — Darius III — fled from a 25-year-old Macedonian on a horse. Alexander didn't even slow down.

208 years. Chest of silver. Gone.

Belly & Thighs of Bronze

"A third kingdom of bronze, which shall rule over all the earth" — Daniel 2:39

Greece. 331–168 BC.

One guy. Alexander the Great. 25 years old, mass-conquered the known world in about a decade. Then died at 32. Just... died. Fever in Babylon.

His empire split four ways between his generals — like breaking a table into four wobbly pieces. None of them were him.

Bronze. Harder than silver, cheaper than gold. Greece gave the world philosophy, democracy, the Olympics — and military campaigns that left a trail of blood across three continents.

Belly and thighs. The core. The engine. But still — just another section of the statue.

Legs of Iron

"Strong as iron — for iron breaks and shatters all things" — Daniel 2:40

Rome. 168 BC – 476 AD. Six hundred years of iron.

Rome didn't just conquer. Rome crushed. Built roads over the rubble. Made you speak Latin. Made you pay taxes. Made you grateful for it.

Two legs. The empire eventually split in two — East and West. Constantinople and Rome. Same iron, different legs.

Iron. The strongest metal in the statue. And it still didn't last.

Feet of Iron & Clay

The western leg fell in 476 AD. Barbarians at the gates — literally. The eastern leg held on another thousand years as Byzantium. But Daniel already saw what was next.

Ten toes. Ten kingdoms. When Rome fell, it didn't become one thing — it shattered into tribal nations:

The Franks. The Visigoths. The Vandals. The Ostrogoths. The Lombards. The Burgundians. The Heruli. The Suevi. The Anglo-Saxons. The Alemanni.

The Franks are the ones who produced Charlemagne. Called himself Emperor.

But Daniel already said it:

"They will mingle with the seed of men; but they will not adhere to one another, just as iron does not mix with clay." — Daniel 2:43

Every attempt to reunite Europe — marriage alliances, Napoleon, the EU — iron and clay. They don't stick.

And we're still here. Still in the feet. Iron and clay — for over 1,500 years now.

Rome was called the Eternal City. The early church believed that when Rome fell, the world itself would perish. They couldn't imagine anything after Rome.

But Daniel could. He saw what came after the iron — not another empire. Just... pieces. Fragments. Toes that don't stick together. And that's exactly what we've been living in ever since 476 AD.

We are the feet.

The Rock of Ages

"A stone was cut out without hands" — Daniel 2:34

Not made by humans. Not voted in. Not built by committee. Not cut by human hands.

That phrase echoes all over the New Testament. Listen for it:

"We have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens." — 2 Corinthians 5:1

"My kingdom is not of this world." — John 18:36

"Our citizenship is in heaven." — Philippians 3:20

"And raised us up with Him, and seated us with Him in the heavenly places." — Ephesians 2:6

"On this rock I will build My church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it." — Matthew 16:18

"Whoever falls on this stone will be broken to pieces; and on whomever it falls, it will grind him to powder." — Matthew 21:44

Jesus knew exactly what He was referencing. Daniel's stone. The one that becomes a mountain and fills the whole earth.

Every kingdom on that statue was built by human hands. This one isn't.

Remember Gideon? A Midianite soldier dreamed that a loaf of barley bread — bread — tumbled into the camp and flattened a tent. His friend said: that's the sword of Gideon. God has delivered us into his hand. (Judges 7:13)

A loaf of bread toppling a military camp. A stone toppling a statue. God's weapons are always underwhelming on paper.

And as the stone is being cut out — more and more people are added to His number. Born again. Called out.

The word "church" in Greek is ekklesia — literally, "the called-out ones."

"You are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special people." — 1 Peter 2:9

And right now, on this earth, they are sojourners. Strangers passing through.

"They desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for He has prepared a city for them." — Hebrews 11:16

Iconoclasm!

"Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver and the gold were broken to pieces and became like chaff on a threshing floor in the summer." — Daniel 2:35

Chaff. That's the image. Not rubble. Not ruins. Chaff. The worthless husk that blows away and nobody goes looking for.

That image shows up everywhere in Scripture when God talks about the wicked:

"The wicked are not so, but are like chaff that the wind drives away." — Psalm 1:4

"Let them be like chaff before the wind, with the angel of the LORD driving them away." — Psalm 35:5

"His winnowing fork is in His hand, and He will clear His threshing floor, gathering the wheat into the barn and burning up the chaff with unquenchable fire." — Matthew 3:12 (John the Baptist)

Gold. Silver. Bronze. Iron. Clay. All of it — chaff. Wind took it. Gone. No trace left.

Jesus told a parable. A nobleman went to a far country to receive a kingdom. Before he left, he gave his servants money to invest. Some of them said: "We do not want this man to reign over us."

When he returned as king, he said:

"But those enemies of mine who did not want me
to be king over them — bring them here
and kill them before me."

— Luke 19:27

That's the stone. That's what it does.

And it's not gradual. It's sudden.

"Behold, I am coming quickly." — Revelation 22:12

The stone doesn't slowly erode the statue. It strikes. One hit. Done.

"The LORD is a man of war." — Exodus 15:3

The rider on the white horse — eyes like a flame of fire, and on His robe and on His thigh a name written:

KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS

— Revelation 19:16

A mighty deliverance.

And then — fire. Just like the flood wiped the world clean in Noah's day, fire finishes the job:

"The heavens will pass away with a great noise, and the elements will melt with fervent heat; both the earth and the works that are in it will be burned up." — 2 Peter 3:10

"And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord will consume with the brightness of His coming." — 2 Thessalonians 2:8

Consumed by the brightness. Not by an army. Not by a battle plan. Just — His presence.

"When You awake, You shall despise their image as a dream." — Psalm 73:20

Despised like a dream. All of it. Every empire, every throne, every monument — like waking up and forgetting what you dreamed about.

And remember the parable of the wheat and the tares? An enemy sowed weeds among the wheat. The servants asked, should we pull them up? The master said — no. Let them grow together until the harvest.

"Let both grow together until the harvest... gather the tares and bind them in bundles to burn them, but gather the wheat into my barn." — Matthew 13:30

And then Jesus explains what happens after:

"Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father." — Matthew 13:43

Starting fresh. Building from the ashes. Literally.

"Then comes the end, when He delivers the kingdom to God the Father, when He puts an end to all rule and all authority and power." — 1 Corinthians 15:24

The Return of the King

"And the stone that struck the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth." — Daniel 2:35

A stone becomes a mountain. Sound familiar? Jesus told a parable about a mustard seed — the smallest seed, becomes the biggest tree. Same pattern. The kingdom starts small. Then it grows. And grows. And fills everything.

Now that all the kingdoms are destroyed — imagine it.

Imagine the beautiful lands of Russia without the communism. The hills of China without the surveillance. The streets of every nation — without the tyrants.

"Destructions are come to a perpetual end." — Psalm 9:6

"They shall not hurt nor destroy in all My holy mountain." — Isaiah 11:9

"They shall not learn war anymore." — Isaiah 2:4

One ruler. One king. The whole world.

"The government shall be upon His shoulders." — Isaiah 9:6

And here's what makes this king different from every king on that statue. Alexander's father was a king. His mother was a queen. Nebuchadnezzar was born into royalty. Caesar was raised for power.

Jesus? His dad was a carpenter.

And yet — one substance with the Father and the Son. Not mixed like iron and clay. Not alloyed. Not fragile. One. Completely unified. Forever.

Imagine a kingdom where every single citizen is a child of the king.

A kingdom of love. The capital — the New Jerusalem.

"Of the increase of His government and peace there will be no end." — Isaiah 9:7

The old kingdoms won't just be gone. They'll be SO gone that "not even the remembrance of them will come up."

A clean slate. The world wiped completely clean.

What was that show we used to watch? Who knows. Who cares. All the accumulated garbage in our minds — gone. Alexander the who? All their glories — like nothing.

"Since there will be found no more place for them." — Daniel 2:35

A complete remaking of the entire earth. The blueprints of the mansions are already drawn.

The rock from a mountain became a mountain.
Thy kingdom come, thy will be done,
on earth as it is in heaven.

When Christ comes it will be: epic 🤩

So What?

Know how worthless the things of this world are — because of how worthless they will be. Chaff in the wind. Washed away like sand at the shore.

"Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy..." — Matthew 6:19

Don't lay up treasures on an earth that the stone will destroy.

Estimate things based on what they will be in the end. Gold, silver, bronze, iron — in the end, it's all dust. Value things accordingly.

Strengthen your alliance with the coming King. Every other king on that statue is gone. This one is coming. Pick your side now.
